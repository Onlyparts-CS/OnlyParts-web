02 Hosting — cheap first, and it scales

One constraint decides the whole shape of this, and it is not price: CERT-In requires 180 days of logs held inside Indian jurisdiction. Combined with the RBI's rule that payment data stays in India, that rules out the reflexive answer of a US-region PaaS and a Frankfurt database. Pick an India region on day one and the compliance question never reopens.

Phase 0 — launch ≈ $55/mo · ₹4,800

LayerChoiceCost/moWhy this one
EdgeCloudflare Pro$20 WAF, DDoS absorption, bot rules and cache. The single highest-value line item here — it is also most of your "cyber compliance" story.
AppDigitalOcean droplet, Bangalore (BLR1) — 2 vCPU / 4 GB$24 India region, so latency and log residency are both solved. Runs the Next standalone build in Docker.
DatabaseDO Managed Postgres, BLR1$15 Backups and point-in-time recovery you do not have to operate. Same region as the app.
ImagesCloudflare R2~$1 Zero egress fees. For an image-heavy parts catalogue this is the difference between a predictable bill and a surprise.
PaymentsRazorpay or Cashfree2% + GST Per transaction, not monthly. Keeps card data entirely off your infrastructure, which is how you stay out of PCI-DSS scope.

This comfortably serves 20,000–40,000 page views a day with Cloudflare caching the catalogue. That is well past where you will be at launch.

Phase 1 — when traffic is real ≈ $150/mo · ₹13,000

Triggered by sustained CPU above 60% or p95 response over 800 ms — not by a date. Add a second app droplet behind a DO load balancer ($12), a managed Redis for sessions and rate-limit counters ($15), and a Postgres read replica ($15). Nothing about the application changes; this is the same architecture with more of it.

Phase 2 — AWS Mumbai ≈ $400/mo · ₹35,000

ECS Fargate, RDS Multi-AZ, Secrets Manager, CloudFront — the layout your own infra conventions already describe. Move when you need multi-AZ failover or a compliance auditor asks for it, and not before. Migrating early costs roughly eight times the bill to solve problems you do not have.

This supersedes docs/10-INFRA-DEVOPS.md — here is why

That document proposes Vercel for the storefront and a Hostinger or Hetzner CX32 VPS for the API, at roughly $35/mo. It is cheaper than the above and it was sound reasoning. Two things have changed since:

  • It describes an architecture that was not built. It splits a NestJS API, Meilisearch and Postgres onto their own VPS behind a separate Vercel storefront. What actually exists is one Next.js + Payload application against one Postgres. Most of the split, and its latency argument, no longer applies.
  • Hetzner is in Europe. CERT-In requires 180 days of logs held inside Indian jurisdiction, which an EU VPS cannot satisfy without a second log pipeline. India to Falkenstein is also a 130–150 ms round trip on every uncached request.

So the real trade is $35/mo with a compliance gap and EU latency, against $55/mo with neither. At ₹1,700 a month of difference, the India region wins. Everything else in that document — Cloudflare in front, R2 for media, Hostinger keeping the registrar and pointing nameservers at Cloudflare — still stands and is assumed here.

03 Cyber compliance

Two regimes apply, and they are unusually specific about mechanics — which is good news, because it means you can actually finish them rather than gesture at them.

CERT-In Directions (in force since April 2022)

RequirementWhat it means in practice
Report incidents within 6 hours The clock starts at awareness, not confirmation. GDPR gives 72 hours; this gives six. You need a named person, a template, and CERT-In's contact details written down before you need them.
180 days of logs, stored in India Application, access and system logs. This is the requirement that picks your hosting region.
Clocks synced to NIC or NPL NTP One line in the server config. Trivial, and specifically named in the directions.

Non-compliance runs to ₹1 lakh and up to a year's imprisonment. Small money, real teeth.

DPDP Act & Rules — the dates are now fixed

Updates docs/16-DPDP-COMPLIANCE.md

That document is a good engineering treatment of the DPDP Act, and it predates the Rules. Its controls, lawful bases and penalty table all still hold — what it could not have is the compliance calendar below, which only became real in November 2025. Read that one for the "what to build"; this section is the "by when".

The Rules were notified on 14 November 2025 with a phased runway. This is the one piece of Indian tech regulation with a genuine deadline you can plan against:

DateWhat landsStatus
Nov 2025Data Protection Board established; its rules effective immediatelyDone
Nov 2026Consent-manager obligations (Rule 4) take effect3 months
13 May 2027Full compliance — every core Data Fiduciary obligation21 months

Penalties scale to ₹250 crore for failing to maintain reasonable security safeguards, ₹200 crore for not notifying a breach, ₹50 crore for anything else. You are a long way from being a Significant Data Fiduciary, so the audit and DPIA obligations do not bite — but consent, notice, breach notification and the right to erasure all do.

What the code already gets right

The account screen's export button is wired to a real server action gated on a signed cookie, and deletion is deliberately absent with the reason written down: a GST invoice must be retained six years, so orders can be anonymised but never deleted. That is the correct reading. It needs finishing once sign-in is real — anonymise-in-place, not delete.

04 What makes it legal to sell these particular things

This is the section that gets skipped, and it is the one with the most exposure — because a parts catalogue is not a generic e-commerce catalogue. Batteries, wireless transmitters and electronics each carry their own regulator.

Legal Metrology — already overdue

In force since 1 July 2026

The Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Amendment Rules 2026 insert sub-rule 10A: every e-commerce entity selling imported products must offer a searchable and sortable country-of-origin filter. Nearly everything in this catalogue is imported. The schema has no origin field at all, so this is a data problem before it is a UI problem — and the suppliers do not publish it, so it has to come off your own import documents.

Rule 6(1) separately requires each listing to show: manufacturer/packer/importer name and address, net quantity, MRP inclusive of all taxes, country of origin, date of manufacture, and consumer-care contact details. Of those, the schema currently has none.

Product-specific approvals

If you sell…You needNotes
Lithium cells and packsBIS registration under CRS IS 16046 Part 2 for cells, Part 1 for packs. Testing must be in an Indian lab — foreign test reports are not accepted. Affects every drone, EV and power drawer.
Batteries of any chemistryEPR registration with CPCB Under the Battery Waste Management Rules, before you put anything on the market. Carries annual collection and recycling targets. 4–8 weeks.
Electronics generallyBIS CRS + E-Waste EPR The CRS order is blunt: no manufacture, sale or distribution of covered electronics without registration.
FPV video transmitters, RF modulesWPC / ETA approval The genuinely risky drawer. Many common FPV VTX units transmit on bands that are not licence-exempt in India at all.
A strategic read, not a legal one

These approvals attach to whoever puts goods on the market. Buying stock and reselling it makes that you. Operating as a marketplace where a registered seller ships to the customer moves most of this burden onto them — and, separately, the marketplace model is the only one of the two that permits foreign investment, which matters the day you raise. Middleman-with-inventory is the model that inherits every one of these obligations. Worth deciding deliberately rather than by default.

Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules 2020

Cheap to satisfy and commonly missed: publish seller details, a clear returns and refunds policy, and a named Grievance Officer with contact details who acknowledges within 48 hours and resolves within one month. These are page-level content changes, not engineering.

05 Trademark and entity

File before launch, not after. Indian trademark rights follow use, but registration is what makes them enforceable — and a marketplace competitor noticing your name after you have spent on marketing is the expensive version of this.

ClassCoversPriority
35Retail and online marketplace services — bringing goods together for others to buyFile first. This is the store itself.
9Electronics, boards, sensors, batteriesFile if you ever brand your own parts
7Machines and machine parts — bearings, motorsOptional at launch
6Common metal hardware — fasteners, fittingsOptional at launch

Cost: ₹4,500 per class filing online as an individual, or as an entity with Udyam/MSME registration or DPIIT startup recognition. ₹9,000 per class for a company or LLP without it. Get the Udyam registration first — it is free and it halves this. Attorney fees typically add ₹5,000–10,000 per class. Expect 12–24 months to registration; you may use ™ from the day you file, ® only after it is granted.

Two real risks with this specific name
  1. Descriptiveness. "OnlyParts" describes what the shop sells, and marks that describe their own goods draw objections under Section 9 of the Trade Marks Act as lacking distinctiveness. The stylised logo is far more defensible than the words — file the device mark (logo + wordmark together), not the plain word.
  2. OnlyScrews. An existing Indian brand, same construction, adjacent goods, and one of the two sites you are pulling catalogue data from. An examiner could reasonably cite it. Pay for a proper search on the IP India register before you file, and before you print anything.

Registrations to do in order

  1. Entity — Private Limited if you intend to raise; LLP or proprietorship if not.
  2. Udyam (MSME) — free, instant, and halves the trademark fee.
  3. GST registration — mandatory for interstate e-commerce supply regardless of turnover.
  4. Trademark, Class 35 — device mark, after a clearance search.
  5. Shops & Establishments — state-level, wherever you operate from.
  6. EPR with CPCB — before the first battery or electronic item ships.

07 The supplier feed, as it now stands

Built and working. tools/feeds/pull.mjs pulls both sources into importer-shaped CSV, and a robu batch has been through the real dry run.

  • Incremental by default — the sitemap's lastmod against a state file, so a nightly run fetches what changed instead of re-walking 110,000 pages.
  • Chunked output — the importer's dry run parses in the browser, so a 110,000-row CSV is a hung tab. 2,000 rows per file, and a chunk is the unit of recovery.
  • robots.txt checked, not assumed — the run aborts rather than continuing to hammer a path that has since been disallowed.
  • Gaps reported out loud — unmapped categories, estimated weights, and every row missing a country of origin are counted at the end of each run.
The honest limit

Scraping is the easy fifth of this. The importer requires an HSN code, a shipping weight and a category in our taxonomy for every new product, and no supplier publishes the first, neither publishes country of origin, and the third is a judgement call. The first version of the category mapper filed a can of spray paint as a drone ESC, because "Fluorescent" contains "esc" — it now matches whole words and has a self-check, but keyword matching mis-files by nature. Rows guessed from a title are flagged separately for exactly this reason. The highest-value next step is a classifier pass over the titles to fill the mapping properly; that is where a multi-agent setup genuinely earns its place, and it is not the scraping.
